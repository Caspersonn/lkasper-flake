### Discovery, full-track playback and keeping favorites

Use scheduled Aurral Flows combining Discover, Trending and Focus sources for music outside the owned library, with one song per artist. Manage the recipe, schedule, quality tiers and automatic downloads from the repository. Provide an initial discovery recipe that does not depend on existing listening history; artist seeds, follows and listening history can refine later runs. Following an artist must not implicitly request its entire discography.

Enable automatic downloads through slskd. A prepared flow contains readable full audio files, not only metadata or Deezer previews. Soulseek availability and peer queues can delay preparation; expose waiting, failed and retry states instead of claiming every recommendation is immediately playable. Rotation is scheduled, not a promise of infinitely generated instant radio. After selecting a prepared flow, the client must play its available full tracks consecutively without visiting an administration UI.

Use Aurral's built-in player and its own `/rest` Subsonic endpoint as the initial playback integration. Verify the built-in player's explicit Add to library action and the Subsonic `Favorite flow tracks` setting, enabled declaratively, against a pinned version. A Subsonic favorite must retain the downloaded flow track in the permanent library before rotation can remove its temporary copy. Do not assume that a heart in every UI has this meaning. Test repeated favorite actions, concurrent rotation, adding a flow track to a static playlist, restart recovery and unfavoriting: removing a favorite or playlist entry must not implicitly delete the retained file.

Navidrome remains available and optional. Publishing Aurral playlists requires compatible Navidrome APIs, shared readable paths and indexing before playlist entries can play. The documented favorite-to-library trigger applies to clients connected to Aurral, not automatically to clients connected directly to Navidrome. Select supported Linux and Android clients only after verifying full-flow playback and favorites against Aurral's Subsonic endpoint; preserve existing client settings.

The pinned Aurral version retains the audio but stores a favorite against the original flow entry; deleting that flow makes the favorite disappear after restart. A repository patch preserves the favorite through an internal alias to its permanent library job, resolving to the indexed library identity when available. Existing request authorization and accepted Subsonic identifiers remain unchanged; this introduces neither a public protocol identifier nor a schema migration. The pinned source file is patched reproducibly and mounted read-only into the pinned image. Clean local and VM full-FLAC fixtures passed favorite/unfavorite, native flow deletion, removal of the unkept file and favorite/playback recovery after restart. VM `/nix/store/8x08n9898zwx8pqa695mc0qj74ajp10y-vm-test-run-aurral-flows-provision` also passed explicit Add to library, saving a flow as a static playlist, concurrent favorite/flow deletion, subsequent static-playlist deletion and restart with both full tracks retained.

### Separate local music storage and direct Soulseek acquisition

Plan an isolated Aurral library at `/data/music-aurral`, temporary flow files under its `_flows` directory, and slskd staging under `/data/downloads/slskd`. Confirm these paths and existing service state before implementation. All participating services must see identical absolute paths and receive only the required shared-media permissions. Aurral imports completed slskd files into its managed storage; do not make either service own or reorganize existing `/data/media/music` or Navidrome libraries as part of bootstrap.

This explicitly permits local full music files and a second, non-torrent acquisition service. It supersedes the earlier all-media-debrid and no-local-music-copy restrictions for this music workflow only. Video remains linked to Real-Debrid. Soulseek peers and the Soulseek service can see the connecting public IP; Real-Debrid does not mediate this traffic. No torrent client, VPN subscription or claim of provider/peer anonymity is introduced. Do not enable YouTube, Usenet or other fallback sources implicitly. Peer-listening ports and any upload/share paths require an explicit repository-managed policy before activation; no existing library is automatically shared.

Define disk budgets, minimum free space, incomplete-transfer cleanup and flow retention before implementation. Rotation may reclaim expired unkept flow files, but must preserve permanent tracks, other active references and pre-existing libraries. Low disk space or downloader outage must stop new acquisition visibly rather than deleting kept music. Keep temporary files separate from permanent retention in backup and restore rules: flow audio can be regenerated, while permanent local audio requires a defined backup/restore policy in addition to application state. Same-server encrypted backups still do not protect against disk loss.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- One shared `/data` tree with identical paths on the host and in every container, with isolated linked video, local Aurral music and downloader staging paths readable by the appropriate services.
- Private-only management interfaces enforced by the NixOS firewall, not by convention.
- No-clickops provisioning from repository-managed settings and protected runtime secrets, including on a fresh installation.
- Home Manager manages supported user application configuration and dotfiles; NixOS manages server services and their configuration.
- Preserve existing services and library data until the replacement passes end-to-end acceptance.
- Everything removable by dropping the imports from kaito.

**Non-Goals:**
- Removing existing libraries or services before replacement acceptance.
- Requiring YouTube or paid streaming as a substitute for music acquisition.
- Local torrent clients (including Transmission), a torrent VPN, Usenet, Prowlarr, FlareSolverr.
- Reusable multi-host defaults; the modules are written for kaito's layout and are x86_64-tested only (both images also publish arm64, but server-casper is not a target).

## Decisions

### NixOS services and Home Manager dotfiles
Keep shared daemons, containers, mounts, firewall rules and service-owned `/var/lib` state in NixOS modules. Add supported client packages and non-secret dotfiles through `flake.modules.homeManager` under `modules/users/shared/`, composed into explicitly selected user profiles. `modules/users/shared/meridian.nix` demonstrates `xdg.configFile` management, and `modules/users/casper/home-manager.nix` composes shared modules.
Web applications may have no local dotfiles; their settings still belong in repository-managed server provisioning. Selected client profiles are `casper@technative-casper`, `casper@gaming-casper` and `casper@personal-casper`. Wire client imports only into those profiles, including their NixOS-integrated Home Manager equivalents; do not add unconditional client imports to the shared Casper module used by other hosts.
*Alternative:* move the entire stack into Home Manager. Not selected because shared mounts, system accounts, containers and firewall belong to the host lifecycle rather than a login profile.

### Declarative provisioning replaces first-run setup
Manage non-secret intent in Nix and keep secret inputs encrypted with agenix. Prefer supported configuration files and secret-file inputs. For API-only settings, use systemd-managed idempotent reconciliation with runtime credentials, readiness checks, bounded retries and explicit failures. Reconcile only owned settings; preserve library records, listening history and user activity.
Bootstrap must cover owner accounts and API credentials: a script requiring a key first created in a web UI does not meet the requirement. Verify every bootstrap mechanism against its pinned version. Render secret-bearing configuration outside the Nix store with restricted permissions, never into derivation arguments or logs. Rebuilds must converge managed settings without duplicating integrations.
*Alternatives:* manual wizards, manual API commands or SQLite edits. Not selected because they do not provide the required reproducible lifecycle.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [HolyArrs with Decypharr is untested end to end; categories and magnet redirects may break grabs] → Test a cached movie and provision required categories declaratively; a different downloader requires a revised design, not a manual workaround.
- [HolyArrs may hash a whole file through the link on rename or delete (issue #158)] → Accept for the test; watch Real-Debrid traffic during renames.
- [Soulseek availability, peer queues and file quality vary] → Prepare full-file flows before playback, validate matches, expose retries and keep retained tracks independent of rotation. Metadata or preview availability does not demonstrate full audio acquisition. Source changes must be repository-managed; no implicit fallback is permitted.
- [Soulseek is direct peer-to-peer and local audio consumes disk] → Declare peer/share policy and bounded temporary storage before activation; protect kept files and existing libraries and document public-IP exposure without claiming Real-Debrid privacy for this path.
- [Public trackers behind Cloudflare need FlareSolverr] → Use indexers that work without it for the test; add FlareSolverr later if needed.
- [HolyArrs versions and APIs evolve] → Pin versions, change pins in Git, and retest bootstrap, integrations and recovery before rollout.
- [`SYS_ADMIN` + `/dev/fuse` widen the Decypharr container's privileges] → Only on kaito, only reachable over the mesh, image pinned.
- [`HOLYARRS_ALLOWED_HOSTS` value format is not documented in detail] → If the hostname is rejected, use `http://10.123.0.1:6262`, which works without it.
- [Fixed uid/gid 2000 could collide on kaito] → Check `getent passwd 2000` / `getent group 2000` on kaito before deploying.

## Migration Plan

1. Resolve the implementation gates and update the concrete design and application-specific tasks before implementation. Preserve historical baseline results separately from the new acceptance criteria.
2. Build affected NixOS and Home Manager configurations. Test unattended fresh installation and repeated activation in a disposable VM before touching kaito.
3. With explicit deployment approval, copy and activate the configuration on kaito; provisioning runs automatically after required secrets and services are ready. Running a deployment command is not web configuration.
4. Test a movie and show through Moonfin request-to-playback, and an Aurral mixed-artist flow through slskd acquisition, full-song playback, favorite-to-library retention and rotation. Verify restart, mount-loss, rebuild and recovery behaviour for the applicable storage paths.
5. Keep Soundspan, Navidrome, AIOStreams and original libraries available throughout migration. Decommission only after equivalent replacement workflows pass and removal is approved.
6. **Rollback:** activate the previous generation or remove newly introduced imports and rebuild, retaining state, keys and original media data. Do not delete state during rollback.

## Moonfin video client and native HolyArrs requests

The Linux FHS environment includes Wayland client and server libraries. The gaming-casper launch reported a missing `libwayland-server.so.0`; the package supplies it through `wayland` rather than requiring host library installation. The earlier Xvfb check did not establish startup on the user's Wayland session.

The selected video client is Moonfin on Linux, Android and Android/Google TV. Jellyfin remains the playback server. Discovery, movie requests, whole-show or selected-season requests and request status belong inside Moonfin rather than a separate administration UI.

Moonfin currently uses the Moonbase authenticated Seerr protocol for requests. The approved integration extends Moonbase with a native HolyArrs backend behind that existing client protocol; it does not deploy Seerr, Radarr or Sonarr. HolyArrs remains responsible for requests and the existing Jackett/Decypharr/Real-Debrid acquisition path. The Jellyfin session authenticates the user, server-side authorization controls acquisition, and the HolyArrs key remains a protected runtime credential. Unsupported protocol operations must return explicit errors. Request approval or metadata presence must not be represented as playable media; availability must be checked against the Jellyfin library.

Moonbase source is pinned and patched in the repository. Client and server compatibility, clean installation, repeat activation, authorization and real movie/TV request-to-playback acceptance require verification before this integration is described as ready. Code comments are not needed.

The implementation uses Jellyfin session validation against its loopback API and fails closed for disabled accounts or accounts with parental, tag or library restrictions; those policies cannot safely be translated through HolyArrs' shared owner credential. Requests are attributed to that managed HolyArrs owner, not to individual Jellyfin accounts. Restricted accounts are rejected rather than supported. Native discovery currently exposes one page of at most 40 results per row. Unsupported management, calendar and queue operations are not advertised. Native genre names are not assigned fabricated TMDB identifiers, and absent release dates remain absent.

Moonbase is pinned to `c6aad40f61a89be84a272329731b5b688eac8567` and installed with its SharpCompress dependency before Jellyfin starts. The HolyArrs key is delivered through a systemd credential, not the plugin configuration or client. Moonfin 3.1.0 is packaged from the official Linux x86-64 release in an FHS environment and selected by the three Casper Home Manager profiles. Android and Android TV use their respective official 3.1.0 APKs; device execution remains acceptance work. Existing client accounts and settings are not overwritten; automatic server registration is not implemented.

Availability enumerates user-visible Jellyfin items in 250-item pages, matches their explicit TMDB provider identifier and checks file readability. Jellyfin 10.11 does not accept `AnyProviderIdEquals` on its items endpoint, so relying on that parameter can incorrectly mark another title playable. Per-request library snapshots avoid enumerating the entire library for each discovery tile. A show is marked partially available only when an indexed episode is readable. VM checks cover absent versus present movie identifiers, restricted and invalid sessions, repeat activation and streaming fixture bytes. They do not establish real acquisition or Android/TV playback.
